Tatyana Sitnova

Researcher in stellar spectroscopy, non-LTE line formation, and Galactic archaeology

I use high-resolution stellar spectra and non-LTE spectral line formation to investigate the origin of chemical elements and reconstruct the chemical evolution of the Milky Way and its satellites.

Research

My research focuses on the origin of the chemical elements, with particular emphasis on neutron-capture nucleosynthesis, very metal-poor stars, and Galactic archaeology.

The source of light neutron-capture elements in the early Galaxy: s-process in massive stars

I use high-resolution spectra of very metal-poor stars for determination of barium isotope ratios together with non-LTE abundances of Sr, Ba, and Eu. These measurements provide the first observational evidence that an early s-process operating in massive stars contributed to the production of Sr in the early Galaxy, resolving a long-standing problem.

Unlocking the mystery of strontium synthesis in the early Galaxy through analysis of barium isotopes in very metal-poor stars
The Ba Isotopic Ratio as a Way of Distinguishing the R- and S-Process in Chemical Evolution Models

Tracing the origin of neutron-capture elements in carbon enhanced metal-poor (CEMP) stars

I use barium isotope ratios together with non-LTE abundances of Ba and Eu to distinguish contributions from the s-, r-, and i-processes in CEMP stars. This provides the observational diagnostic capable of unambiguously separating i-process enrichment from s-process enrichment.

Distinct barium isotope ratios in CEMP-s and CEMP-rs stars
Ba Isotope Ratio in CEMP-s and CEMP-rs Stars as a Signature of s-Process and i-Process

Galactic archaeology

I use high-resolution spectroscopy of stars in stellar streams and dwarf galaxies to reconstruct their chemical evolution and formation histories.
